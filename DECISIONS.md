# DECISIONS.md — Calendar-Year Plan Loss Ratio Workbook

Every judgment call made while building this tool, and why. Section references (§) point to the
build brief (`plan_lr_workbook_claude_code_prompt.md`).

---

## 1. Method restatement (in my own words)

A rate level indication produces a projected loss ratio `LR_proj` for the policy year written
around the indication's assumed effective date, 7/1/(P−1). Because an annual policy year effective
7/1/(P−1) has its average loss date at 7/1/P — the midpoint of plan year P — the indication's loss
projection is already trended to the plan year's loss center of gravity, so **no additional loss
trend is applied for the plan-year result**.

The premium side is where the bases differ. The indication's loss ratio assumes premium at the
indication's cumulative rate level `CRL_ind` (the product of all rate changes the indication
considered), **fully earned**. The calendar year P will not actually earn premium at that level:
old rates still earn in during the early months (carryover), and planned changes earn in only
partially. The engine therefore builds, month by month:

1. A **written rate index** `W_k` for each monthly writing cohort `k` — the cumulative product of
   all rate changes effective on or before that cohort's writing date (base 1.000 before the first
   logged change). Planned changes contribute `filed % × achievement %`.
2. An **earning matrix** `e(k, m)` — how much of cohort `k`'s premium earns in calendar month `m`,
   using the standard mid-month convention for a `T`-month policy term: `1/(2T)` in the writing
   month, `1/T` in each of the next `T−1` months, `1/(2T)` in month `k+T`.
3. The **calendar-year earned rate index** — the exposure-weighted aggregate
   `E_CY(Y) = Σ_m Σ_k w_k·e(k,m)·W_k / Σ_m Σ_k w_k·e(k,m)`, with `w_k` the written weight
   (uniform by default, optional monthly seasonality).

The **rate adjustment factor** is `A_rate(P) = CRL_ind / E_CY(P)`: it restates the projected loss
ratio from "indication rate level, fully earned" to "what CY P actually earns."

Schedule mod drift is the same economics through a different lever: the average schedule mod is a
multiplicative premium modifier fixed at issuance, so drift in the average written mod earns in on
written business exactly like rate. The written mod path `M_w(k)` is piecewise-linear through the
anchor points supplied by the user; the earned mod `M̄_E(Y)` is the exposure-weighted **average**
(not product — mods are levels) through the same earning matrix, and
`A_mod(P) = M_ind / M̄_E(P)`.

The bridge is then:

```
LR_current = LR_input × (1 + s)   if input basis is 'proposed', else LR_input
CY_LR(P)   = LR_current × A_rate(P) × A_mod(P) × A_other
CY_LR(P+1) = LR_current × (1 + net_trend) × A_rate(P+1) × A_mod(P+1) × A_other   [indicative]
```

Directionality intuition (encoded as property tests): a planned increase the indication did not
consider adds earned premium the indication's LR never contemplated ⇒ `A_rate < 1` (CY LR below
projected). A considered change that CY P has not fully earned means CY premium sits *below* the
indication's fully-earned level ⇒ pushes `A_rate > 1` (carryover shortfall). Mods rising above
`M_ind` ⇒ more premium than the indication assumed ⇒ `A_mod < 1`.

Hand verification of the §9 worked example (done before any code):
`CRL_ind = 1.04 × 1.06 = 1.1024`. CY-2027 parallelogram areas for annual policies, uniform
writings: 0.125 @ 1.0400 (pre-7/1/2026 rates), 0.59375 @ 1.1024, 0.28125 @ 1.157520
(areas sum to 1.000) → `E_CY = 1.1101025` → `A_rate = 0.9930617`. Mod path 0.860 (close of
9/30/2026) → 0.890 (close of 12/31/2027) is 15 months at 0.002/month → written mod at 1/1/2027
= 0.866; by the linear-mod identity `M̄_E(2027) = 0.8660` → `A_mod = 0.850/0.866 = 0.9815242`.
`CY LR = 65.0% × 0.9930617 × 0.9815242 = 63.36%`. All stated values reproduced.

## 2. Timeline and convention decisions

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|----------|-----------|
| D1 | **Cohort window extended to 48 months** — Jan (P−2) through Dec (P+1) — rather than the brief's 36 months (§3.2.1). | §3.4.4 requires `E_CY(P−1)` for the year-over-year earned rate change, and CY (P−1) earns from cohorts written as early as Jan (P−2) for a 12-month term. The 36-month window cannot produce `E_CY(P−1)`. Earned months reported: Jan (P−1) – Dec (P+1), 36 months. This is the *only* deviation from a stated §3 parameter, made because the brief is internally inconsistent on this point; everything downstream (earned window, dashboards) matches the brief. |
| D2 | **Rate-change effective dates are start-of-day; mod as-of anchors are close-of-day.** | A change "effective 7/1" applies to business written on 7/1 (per the §3.2.2 pro-rata formula, a day-1 effective date gives the full month the new rate). A mod "as of 9/30/2026" is a measurement at the close of that day, i.e., the 10/1 boundary. This is also the only reading that reproduces the worked example exactly: 9/30/2026→12/31/2027 is then exactly 15 months, giving written mod 0.8660 at 1/1/2027 (start-of-day anchoring gives 0.86607 and misses the stated `A_mod = 0.98152`). In Excel, mod anchor serials are `date + 1`, labeled in-sheet. |
| D3 | **Continuous (closed-form) engine measures time in months** (each month = 1/12 year; intra-month dates pro-rata by day) rather than actual day counts. | Reproduces textbook parallelogram geometry and the brief's stated expected values exactly (7/1 = 0.500, 4/1 = 0.250 of a year). The monthly production engine uses real day counts only where the brief demands it (intra-month pro-rata splits, mod-path interpolation on date serials); differences are tiny and covered by the ±0.10-pt monthly-vs-continuous tolerance. |
| D4 | **Multiple rate changes in one cohort month for the same BU\|LOB**: the written weight is split at the *first* change date between the pre-month index and the full end-of-month index (all changes compounded). Exact when ≤ 1 change per month per combo — the realistic case; a `Checks` warning fires when two changes share a month. | The §3.2.2 pro-rata rule is stated for one change; a fully general intra-month daily average is not expressible in auditable classic-mode Excel. The oracle implements the same convention (plus an exact-daily cross-check used in tests), so Excel ties the oracle exactly. |
| D5 | **Written index / `CRL_ind` share base 1.000 "before all logged changes."** `W_k` is the product of *all* changes effective ≤ cohort date; `CRL_ind` the product over `considered = Y` rows. | `A_rate` is a ratio, so only relative consistency matters. A pre-window change flagged not-considered still (correctly) moves `W` but not `CRL_ind`. |
| D6 | **Mod path beyond the last anchor extends the last segment linearly; before the first anchor, extends the first segment** (the `M_prior` segment when given, else the `M_0→M_1` segment, per §3.3.1). Cohort mod is evaluated at the cohort's actual mid-month date. | Matches §3.3.1; documented on the Mod Engine and Methodology sheets. Cohort mid-months never actually exceed the 12/31/(P+1) anchor within the 48-month window. |
| D7 | **A planned row flagged `considered = Y`** is included in `CRL_ind` at its effective (achievement-adjusted) value, with a warning on `Checks`. | §3.2.7 says validate and warn, not exclude; including it keeps `CRL_ind` equal to "product of flagged rows" (the §9 check) under all inputs. |
| D8 | **Achievement % applies to `planned` rows only**; `taken` rows always use filed % (achievement column ignored, greyed by convention). | §3.1 definition. |

## 3. Architecture decisions

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|----------|-----------|
| D9 | **Excel recalculation via Excel COM automation** (`tools/recalc.py`), since LibreOffice is not installed on the build machine but desktop Excel 16.0 is. The script also supports `soffice --headless` when present. | §9 allows "an equivalent recalc script"; native Excel is the authoritative calc engine, strictly better than a LibreOffice proxy. Classic-mode formulas are still used throughout so LibreOffice validation remains possible anywhere. |
| D10 | **`_calc` engine blocks use a closed-form year-bucket earning column** instead of repeating the 36-column monthly matrix per combo: for cohort `k` and year `Y`, `ec(k,Y) = C(mEnd−k) − C(mStart−1−k)` where `C(o) = MIN(1, MAX(0, (o+0.5)/T))` is the cumulative mid-month earning curve. Mathematically identical to summing `e(k,m)` over `m ∈ Y` (proved in `tests/test_engine.py::test_ec_closed_form_equals_matrix_sums`). | Keeps the hidden per-combo blocks compact (§5.8 "compact engine block") and under the 120-char formula target. The visible `Rate Engine` sheet still shows the full 48×36 matrix for auditability, and a `Checks` row ties the visible matrix aggregate to the compact form for the selected combo — a live cross-method validation. |
| D11 | **Formulas reference fixed named column ranges** (e.g., `rl_eff`, `lr_key`) that exactly cover the input tables, not structured table references (`tbl_RateLog[...]`). Tables are still real Excel Tables for UX; their sizes are fixed (40 rate-log rows, 18 LR rows, 6 seasonality rows) with blank-tolerant formulas. | Structured references recalculate inconsistently in LibreOffice and grow/shrink with table edits, silently breaking fixed engine ranges. Fixed named ranges are classic-mode-safe, auditable by name, and documented on `Methodology`. Spare blank rows are included; the row counts are stated on `Read Me` and `Methodology`. |
| D12 | **Cumulative rate products in Excel via `EXP(SUMPRODUCT(cond × ln1p))`**, where `ln1p = IF(1+r_eff>0, LN(1+r_eff), 0)` is a helper column in `tbl_RateLog`. A `Checks` row flags any `1 + r_eff ≤ 0`, and data validation bounds filed % to [−50%, +200%] and achievement to [0%, 150%] so the guard can never trip with validated inputs. | Classic-mode Excel has no conditional `PRODUCT`. The log-sum identity is exact, blank-row-safe (blank ⇒ ln 0 = 0), and keeps every formula under the length target. Each `IF` guard is documented; `IFERROR` is avoided entirely. |
| D13 | **The Solver's base index `W⁰` uses `taken` rows only** — it solves for a single planned action that *replaces the entire planned program* at the chosen date. | Acceptance §11.7 requires that solving for the worked example's own CY LR return +5.0% at 4/1/2027 — only possible if the +5% planned row itself is excluded. "Replace the planned program with one action" is the cleanest defensible reading of "a single unknown planned change," and is stated prominently on the `Solver` sheet. |
| D14 | **Scenario definitions**: each scenario applies four levers to the *base* inputs of the selected combo — Δ filed points added to every planned row, an effective-date shift in months (via `EDATE`), an achievement override, and a Δ to `M_1` (and `M_2`, so the post-P segment shifts with it). Blank levers = base value; scenario engine blocks live on `_calc`, presentation on `Scenarios`. | Matches §5.9's levers ("planned change magnitude, effective date, achievement, and mod path") with the fewest inputs, applied uniformly so scenarios stay auditable. |
| D15 | **Control's scenario selector** picks which scenario (Base/S1–S4) is surfaced in the Control KPI row (side-by-side with Base); the main engine sheets and Bridge always show Base inputs. | Letting a scenario silently rewire the visible audit path would undermine traceability; the Scenarios sheet is the sanctioned comparison view. |
| D16 | **Control's "LR basis display" toggle** switches the Projected-LR KPI between "as input" and "at current rate level"; the underlying basis remains the per-combo input in `tbl_LR` and the Bridge always normalizes from that input. | §4 makes basis a per-combo input; a global override would corrupt combos entered on different bases. A display toggle preserves the §5.2 feature without that hazard. |
| D17 | **Mod adjustment**: effective switch = per-combo `tbl_LR` toggle AND the global Control master toggle. Same pattern for seasonality (global on/off × per-LOB weights, blank row = uniform). | Both §4 (per-combo/per-LOB inputs) and §5.2 (Control toggles) are honored; AND-composition is the only non-surprising semantics. |
| D18 | **`formula_mode: modern` is validated but not implemented** (config raises a clear error). Classic ships as default and only classic is generated. | §8 says modern "may" be offered. A half-exercised second formula dialect doubles the audit surface for zero methodological value; declining it is the auditable choice. Documented in README as a future hook. |
| D19 | **Attribution actuals**: per planned row (up to 8 slots, auto-pulled for the selected combo), the user enters actual achieved change % and actual effective date; plus actual mod anchors (`M_0ᵃ`/as-of and `M_1ᵃ`, defaulting to plan) and actual CY LR. Decomposition order: (1) rate magnitude at planned dates, (2) rate timing at actual dates, (3) mod drift, (4) loss-side residual — order stated on-sheet (§7 E2 requires the order be documented). Taken rows are assumed booked as logged. | Matches §7 E2's step list; the residual absorbs everything not premium-side, and the reconciliation check proves the factors multiply back to actual. |
| D20 | **No merged cells anywhere** — titles use `centerContinuous` alignment. | §8 bans merges in calc areas; banning them everywhere is simpler and safer (merge-related range corruption is a classic workbook failure mode). |
| D21 | **Portfolio total**: two rows — an EP-weighted total over rows where plan EP is provided, and a simple average over all rows; a footnote states which rows the weighted total covers. All 18 seeded combos ship with sample EP so the weighted row works out of the box. | §5.8 asks for EP-weighted "where provided (simple average with a footnote otherwise)"; showing both makes the fallback permanent and transparent rather than conditional formatting sleight-of-hand. |
| D22 | **Sample data**: all 18 BU×LOB combos are seeded with clearly-marked sample rows; `BU-A | Property` carries the exact §9 worked example and is the default selection. Inland Marine demonstrates the 6-month term override plus a seasonality profile; one combo (`BU-C | Umbrella`) ships with *no* rate-log rows to prove the degenerate path computes factors of 1.000 cleanly; one combo demonstrates achievement < 100%, and one a mid-month effective date. | §4 wants one realistic example row per table; §10 wants the term override demonstrated; §3.5 requires degenerate inputs to compute cleanly — seeding one live degenerate combo keeps that permanently exercised. |
| D23 | **Oracle-tie checks are fingerprint-scoped**: `Checks` compares the workbook's live Bridge to `_oracle` constants only while the worked-example inputs are still present (a fingerprint formula watches the relevant input cells); otherwise those rows report N/A — INPUTS CHANGED. Structural checks (matrix sums, bounds, identities, sanity) are always-on. | Baked constants are only meaningful for the inputs they were computed from; failing loudly on *edited* inputs would train users to ignore red. |
| D24 | **Python source is split** into `src/engine.py` (oracle), `src/build_workbook.py` (generator core + CLI), `src/xlstyle.py` (theme helpers), five focused sheet-builder modules (`sheets_inputs / sheets_engine / sheets_calc / sheets_main / sheets_report`), plus `tools/recalc.py` and `tools/verify_workbook.py`. The 48-cohort engine block is emitted by ONE shared writer (`sheets_engine.write_cohort_block`) everywhere it appears — visible Rate Engine, all 18 `_calc` combo blocks, scenario/attribution/solver variants — so the audited formula pattern is identical throughout. | §10 names the two core files; a single 4,000-line generator would be less auditable, which violates the brief's own tiebreaker. One block writer eliminates the classic failure mode of parallel formula implementations drifting apart. |
| D25 | **Sheet protection not applied** (allowed as optional by §8); stated on `Read Me`. | Protection without passwords adds friction for the maintaining team while providing no integrity guarantee; the Checks panel is the integrity mechanism. |
| D26 | **Solver seed target** is the worked example's own monthly-convention CY LR at full precision, so the shipped workbook demonstrates acceptance §11.7 (returns +5.000% at 4/1/2027) on first open. | Self-demonstrating sample beats a blank input. |
| D27 | **Flow Dashboard "unearned runway"** (§5.7e) is defined as `W_eoq / E_QTD_q − 1`: the written index at quarter end versus the index earned during that quarter — the premium-rate level already locked in on the books but not yet showing in earned premium. Definition printed beside the table. | The brief names but does not define the quantity; this is the standard "carryover runway" planners quote, and it is computable from displayed columns. |
| D28 | **Uniform seasonality normalization**: seasonality weights are used as relative weights (normalized to mean 1 in-engine for display); all engine ratios are normalization-invariant. A blank seasonality row or the global toggle OFF ⇒ uniform. Weights validated ≥ 0, sum > 0. | §3.2.4. Ratios cancel any scale; validation prevents zero-sum degeneracy. |
| D29 | **P+1 mod anchors**: `M_2` defaults to `M_1` (flat beyond 12/31/P) per §3.1; the kink at 1/1/(P+1) is after every cohort mid-month that CY P earns from, so the CY P linear-mod identity is unaffected. Identity check auto-suspends (reports N/A) when its preconditions fail (M_prior supplied and off-line, seasonality on, or T ≠ 12). | §3.3.5 scopes the identity to "default settings"; suspending with an explanation beats false FAILs. |
| D30 | **Rate entry format**: percent-formatted fractions everywhere (5.0% stored as 0.05), consistent across filed %, achievement, trends, LR. Stated on `Read Me` and enforced by validation ranges. | §12 default. |

## 5. Further decisions made during the build

| D32 | **Excel defined names are case-insensitive** — the generator's name registry rejects any two names differing only by case. Found the hard way: `slv_w` (weights) and `slv_W` (index) silently collided, making the Solver read weights where it wanted the index; renamed to `slv_widx` and guarded permanently. | Excel resolves `slv_W` and `slv_w` to the same name; without the guard the failure is silent and data-corrupting. |

| D39 | **Net rate selection (user direction, 2026-07-30; strictly optional, per BU×state).** The dilemma: product sometimes declares an OUTCOME — "we get x% net (rate + schedule-mod price) on business written from 1/1/P; specifics TBD" — rather than a datable program. Entering that as a rate-log row is wrong because log entries are level steps that compound on history (their example: +10% filed 10/1/(P−1) plus a modeled "+10% at 1/1" would stack to +21% in January). **Interpretation adopted**: the selection is a year-over-year renewal target on the combined net price index `q(k) = W(k) × M_w(k)/M_ind` — for cohorts written on/after 1/1/P, `q(k) = q(k−12) × (1+x)`, superseding planned rate rows and the projected mod path *for those cohorts only*; history before 1/1/P earns exactly as modeled (the carryover half of CY P). This reading makes the fall filing the delivery vehicle for Jan–Sep renewals (their expiring policies pre-date it) and — deliberately, visibly in the `Q net` column — compounds on Q4 renewals of already-increased business, which is the literal meaning of an x% net target on renewals. The rejected alternative (a level target vs. some baseline) is exactly a planned row / the Solver, and contradicts "we want to get x% *in* 2027". Mechanics: three columns in every engine block (`q hist`, `Q net` via a 12-rows-up self-reference — no circularity — and `index in force`); every aggregate reads the in-force column, so OFF is bit-identical to classic; the bridge shows one combined factor `A_net = CRL_ind/E[q]` with `A_mod = 1` (rate and price are inseparable under a net target); P+1 has its own optional selection (blank = carry x); with the combo's mod adjustment OFF the target degrades to rate-only (advisory-flagged). Solver and Scenarios' planned-row levers describe the explicit-program counterfactual (on-sheet notes); a fifth scenario lever varies Δ-net; Attribution suspends its premium-side steps for net-mode combos (mixing net and explicit bases would make the factors meaningless). Oracle: `net_sel_p/net_sel_p1` on ComboInputs, monthly recursion + a continuous closed-form analog (history breakpoints shifted +12/+24 keep Simpson exact across the self-referencing path), 10 new tests including the user's exact example. | The user's stated workflow, implemented as an outcome assertion rather than a synthetic rate row — auditable, strictly optional, and exact against the oracle in both conventions. |
